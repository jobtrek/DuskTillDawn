# Idea: generator yard

Parked for M2. Last-resort power.

**Whats there:**
  - Diesel generator in a big metal housing with a control panel, an exhaust stack and a little blinking status light.
  - Fuel tank with a gauge you read by walking up to it.
  - Transfer switch cabinet that decides where the power comes from: solar, battery, grid or generator.
  - Cable run going into the main building. 
  - Fuel delivery spot where a tanker truck parks.
  - Fence and gate around the yard, with a warning sign and a fire extinguisher on the post.

  **The core mechanic:**
    - Solar panels power everything during the day and charge the battery shed.
    - Batteires take over at night or when the sky is cloudy.
    - The grid is the normal backup from the substation.
    - The generator is the last resort when everything else fails. 

**Generator mini-game:**
  - Cold start. Prime the fuel, hold the start button, watch the needle, and let go at the right moment. On cold nights it takes several tries.
  - Refuel. Drag the hose and fill the tank without overflowing. A spill is a fire risk.
  - Manual switchover. If the automatic transfer switch fails, you throw the lever yourself in the right order. Doing it in the wrong order causes a power surge.
  - Laod shedding. The generator can't power everything. You decide which servers stay on and which you shut down, with the main computer telling you what's critical.

 **Incidents for the generator yard:**
  - It won't start. The grid is down, the batteries are draining , and the generator coughs and dies. Causes vary: old fuel, dead starter battery, or cold. You have a countdown until the batteries run out.
  - Empty tank. Someone forgot to refuel after last time. Now you need an emergency fuel delivery at 3am, which ties into the dock and the gate.
  -  Overload. too many servers are running on generatior power. It overheats and trips. You have to shed load fast.
  - Long outrage. A storm takes the grid down for hours. It becomes a survival challange where you plan fuel use across the whole night.
  - Exhaust problem. Fumes build up because the vent is blocked by snow or a bird's nest. You have to clear it before it's dangerous.
  Stolen fuel. The gauge drops overnight for no reason. Someone has been siphoning it. Upgrading the fence or camera fixes it.

 **Upgrades you can unlock:**
  - Automatic monthly test. Runs during the day and warns you about problems before night.
  - Fuel level sensor. Shows the fuel level on the dashboard so you don't have to walk out.
  - Block heater. The generator starts reliably on cold nights.
  - Second generator. Redundancy for bigger companies(LATE GAME). One can fail while the other runs.
  - Smart load shedding. Non-critical servers shut down automatically.

**Day vs Night**
  - Day: run tests, refuel, do maintenance. Skipping it saves time but raises the rish at night.
  - Night: the generator is either completly ignored or it's the most important thing in the game.

**Multiplayer**
  - One player is at the generator doing the cold start. The other is at the main computer watching the battery precentage drop and calling out how much time is left.
  - Load shedding together: the yard player reads out how much power the generator is producing, and the computer player chooses which servers to turn off to match. 
  - Combined incident: grid  failure plus a generator that won't start. One player fixes the generator while the other goes to the baterry shed to stretch its power by switching off non-essential circuits.

 **Extra**
 The generator has moods it rumbles happily when maintained and wheezes when neglected. Players will feel guilty when they skip maintainance, which is exactly the lesson.
